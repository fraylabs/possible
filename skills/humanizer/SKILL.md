# Humanizer

Make the writing sound like someone with a reason to say it. Preserve truth before style.

## Establish the voice source

Read the brief, source copy, decision records, product evidence, founder language, audience language, and claims register. Extract:

- words and sentence patterns the author actually uses;
- concrete product details, decisions, trade-offs, constraints, and sensory observations;
- the audience, channel, desired action, and emotional temperature;
- facts and claims that must remain unchanged;
- phrases that require legal, medical, scientific, or brand review.

When no real voice sample exists, default to clear, restrained language. Do not invent a founder personality, personal history, customer quote, research result, or emotional confession.

## Diagnose before rewriting

Mark passages weakened by:

- abstract openings that delay the point;
- inflated importance, certainty, emotion, or universality;
- generic enthusiasm and interchangeable brand language;
- repetitive three-part lists, sentence rhythms, or conclusions;
- excessive headings, fragments, rhetorical questions, em dashes, and asides;
- stock constructions such as “in today’s world,” “not just X but Y,” “whether you’re,” “game-changing,” “revolutionary,” “seamless,” or “unlock”;
- claims disguised as mood, implication, metaphor, or founder conviction;
- explanations that hide the actual decision, evidence, or trade-off.

Do not remove a phrase merely because it appears on a list. Keep language that is natural in context and supported by the speaker.

## Rewrite

1. Lead with the useful fact, tension, decision, or observation.
2. Replace abstractions with specific nouns, active verbs, measurements, materials, actions, or consequences.
3. Explain important choices through the alternatives rejected, evidence considered, and trade-offs accepted.
4. Vary sentence length and paragraph shape only where meaning benefits.
5. Prefer the shortest wording that preserves the author’s intent and channel requirements.
6. Keep uncertainty visible. Say what is known, inferred, hoped, and still being tested.
7. Preserve deliberate terminology, search terms, accessibility text, disclosures, and calls to action.

For social content, adapt the same truth to the platform instead of pasting one generic voice everywhere. A thread may develop an argument; a carousel may make one decision legible per slide; a film script may leave space for images and sound.

## Verify

Compare the revision against the source and report any material change. Confirm that:

- no fact, number, attribution, quotation, promise, or limitation changed;
- no unsupported health, safety, performance, sustainability, demand, or comparative claim appeared;
- no generated anecdote or synthetic customer language is presented as authentic;
- repeated phrases are intentional;
- the final copy can be read aloud without sounding staged;
- the requested output remains complete.

Do not optimize for AI-detector scores, imitate a living writer, introduce mistakes to appear human, or conceal the use of AI when disclosure is required. The goal is credible writing, not deceptive authorship.
